Choosing an AI-native BI platform

Vendor selectionBusiness caseProcurement

How I found, proved and paid for the AI-native BI platform behind our clients’ Insight Hubs, from the first audit to board sign-off.

The problem

The existing reporting process was costing the business over $1M in salary. This was discovered through an internal audit which found media teams spent 22% of their time on reporting and client teams 16%.

Our dashboards were built on a basic visualisation tool, static and stitched together from sources with different naming conventions. They were reliant on our engineering team being available for amends on a daily basis and weren’t serving a smart way of working.

Window shopping

I ran a structured selection across five platforms, scoring each against seven criteria:

  • At least the functionality of our existing tool
  • Self-serve report building
  • Natural-language AI analytics
  • A forward-looking product roadmap
  • White-labelling
  • Pricing that didn’t scale by user
  • Pricing that didn’t scale by data source

Only one platform met all seven: an AI-native data visualisation tool.

Proving it

Before asking for budget, we ran a two-week proof of concept with internal teams and a pilot client, against ten success criteria:

  • A live connection to our data warehouse, with no duplicated data
  • Self-serve dashboards without data engineering support
  • Clear definitions for every metric
  • Adoption and usage tracking
  • Row-level security for each client and brand
  • Less time spent on reporting
  • Dashboards at least as fast as our existing tool
  • Natural-language questions for people without SQL
  • AI alerts when a metric moves unexpectedly
  • Teams rating it more usable than what we had

It met all ten, including two we could only show through live examples in that window. The feedback from teams was some of the best I’ve seen.

“It’s one of the best things I’ve ever seen.”

A colleague, after the proof of concept

The business case

I built a commercial model that covered the cost in year one and made money in year two. It weighed three options:

  • Absorb it as an investment in our product
  • Pass it on to clients at cost
  • Sell it to clients as a premium Insight Hub

At one new client a month, the model showed an 8% profit margin in year one and a 64% margin in year two, with income at almost three times the cost.

I negotiated a two-year contract, which cut the year-one cost by a third and turned a forecast loss into a profit.

The outcome

The board approved the budget and we rolled the platform out across the agency, white-labelled for clients as their own Insight Hub.

It became one of three company-wide BI and GenAI tools behind our move from 15% to 90% AI adoption.
