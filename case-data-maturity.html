Case study · Data strategy

Turning a data audit into a 12-month technology investment roadmap

Data maturityMeasurementRoadmapping

How auditing a national housebuilder’s data maturity showed where to invest and how to optimise their marketing faster.

The challenge

A national housebuilder was spending on media across hundreds of developments, each with its own lead targets. They wanted to match investment to the needs of every development, prove what media was really driving enquiries, and rely less on expensive property portals.

Their data foundations had been inherited from a previous agency and before investing in new technology, we needed to know what they already had and how well it was being used.

The audit

I led a full audit across nine areas of data maturity, from tracking and feeds to measurement. Each was scored twice: once for the quality of the data, and once for how well it was applied to marketing decisions.

The audit showed a clear pattern: in several areas the data was good, but barely used. Analytics scored well on quality and poorly on application. Feeds, econometrics and lift studies scored poorly on both, which pointed to the biggest gaps in measurement.

From scores to objectives

However, scores on their own don’t make a strategy and we needed to map each gap against the client’s objectives. We simplified seven business goals into four:

  • Market strategy: understanding the media landscape and its opportunities
  • Optimised media: matching investment to the lead needs of each development
  • Measurement framework: joining platform attribution with econometrics
  • Reporting and insights: faster, cheaper reporting for head office and regional teams

The roadmap

That gave us six strategic data projects, prioritised by impact and readiness over the next 12 months.

The quick wins came first: fixing tracking and moving reporting onto an AI-native BI platform. That built the foundations for the bigger measurement projects that followed.

The outcome

Focusing investment where the audit showed the biggest gaps delivered a 20% saving on technology costs against the original plan.

With cleaner data and reporting the whole team could use, learnings moved into marketing optimisation much faster.
