Case study · Operations

Standardising briefing and workflows in a remote business

Change managementOperating modelAdoption

How one rule and a simpler system brought briefing, workload and approvals into one place, and gave leaders a live view of the work.

The problem

Our briefing and workload management processes weren’t working. Briefs were inconsistent and often incomplete, which led to poorer quality work, bigger margins of error and avoidable mistakes that hurt client satisfaction.

In a mostly remote business, team leaders also had little view of who was working on what, or which client work was at risk of running late. That hurt both client and employee satisfaction, and we needed one agency-wide solution.

The mandate

First, we audited how the business and our clients actually worked, and compared it with where we needed to be. That framework for getting from A to B shaped a new operational flow in ClickUp, built around one rule:

“If a task isn’t on ClickUp, it doesn’t exist.”

The ClickUp charter

That single line moved briefing out of inboxes and into one place, with clear deliverables, named owners, dates and priorities.

Simple and standardised briefing

Every brief now uses the same template. Four fields are required: due date, assignee, description and channel. Brief owner, client and team fill in automatically, and a named reviewer picks up the work for QA once it is done.

We cut each client down to one task list, simplified the statuses and set out when not to use ClickUp, so it stayed focused on work, not chat.

Clear statuses. Every task moves through eight statuses in three groups, so anyone can see where work is at a glance. A built-in approval step sends finished work to the brief owner before it is marked complete.

Seeing the work

With every brief in one place, team leaders and the senior leadership team could see workload across the agency for the first time. We built a view for every level. People and clients are anonymised below.

The weekly stand-up. Six questions, in order, for each team member: what is overdue, due, stale, blocked, coming up and undated.

The team view. What each client team has completed, what’s overdue and what’s due today.

My week. Everyone gets their own dashboard, including work they have briefed to others that is running late.

SaaS live status tracking. The same flow tracks the health of every client-facing SaaS tool, so issues are fixed before clients spot them.

The outcome

The charter aligned 150+ people across 7 divisions around one briefing process and a shared communication rhythm.

Team leaders and the senior leadership team gained a live view of workload across the agency.
