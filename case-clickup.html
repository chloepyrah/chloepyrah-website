Case study · Operations

One way of working in ClickUp

Change managementOperating modelAdoption

How one rule and a simpler system brought briefing, workload and approvals into one place, and gave leaders a live view of the work.

The problem

Our briefing and workload management weren’t working. Briefs were inconsistent and often incomplete. In a mostly remote business, team leaders had little view of who was working on what.

That hurt both client and employee satisfaction. We needed one agency-wide solution.

The mandate

First, we audited how the business and our clients actually worked, and compared it with where we needed to be. That framework for getting from A to B shaped a new operational flow in ClickUp, built around one rule:

“If a task isn’t on ClickUp, you don’t need to do anything yet.”

The ClickUp charter

That single line moved briefing out of inboxes and into one place, with clear deliverables, named owners, dates and priorities.

Keeping it simple

Adoption depends on the tool being easy, so we stripped it back:

  • One list per client
  • Four required fields
  • Clear statuses, with a built-in approval step

We also set out when not to use ClickUp, so it stayed focused on work, not chat.

Seeing the work

With every brief in one place, team leaders and the senior leadership team could see workload across the agency for the first time. We built a view for every level. People and clients are anonymised below.

The weekly stand-up. Six questions, in order, for each team member: what is overdue, due, stale, blocked, coming up and undated.

The team view. What each client team has completed, what’s overdue and what’s due today.

My week. Everyone gets their own dashboard, including work they have briefed to others that is running late.

Insight Hubs. The same flow tracks the health of every client Insight Hub, so issues are fixed before clients spot them.

The outcome

The charter aligned 150+ people across 7 divisions around one briefing process and a shared communication rhythm.

Team leaders and the senior leadership team gained a live view of workload across the agency.
